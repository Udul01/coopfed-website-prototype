1.0 Objective

This concept paper proposes the transformation of Sri Lanka’s Consumer Cooperative Societies into a modern chain of supermarkets.

These Cooperative Supermarkets will operate as "One-Stop Department Stores" covering essential segments and services such as groceries, fresh agricultural produce, meat, fish, bakery items, dairy products, pharmacies, cosmetics, household goods, beverages (including legal wine and spirits), and apparel based on consumer demand.

The objective of this proposed reorganization program is to empower cooperative societies to stabilize the prices of essential goods while ensuring food security, in addition to capturing a significant retail market share and providing high-quality products to consumers at affordable prices within a modern environment.

2.0 Background & Context

2.1 Administrative Structure of Sri Lanka: Under the 13th Amendment, nine Provincial Councils were established. Currently, provincial administrative activities are conducted by nine Governors accountable to the President. District administration is conducted by 25 District Secretaries, managing 332 Divisional Secretariat Divisions through Divisional Secretaries. The country consists of 14,022 Grama Niladhari Divisions, 22 Electoral Districts, 29 Municipal Councils, 36 Urban Councils, and 276 Pradeshiya Sabhas.

2.2 Global Cooperatives: Originating with the Rochdale Society of Equitable Pioneers in 1844 by textile workers in Great Britain based on Robert Owen's concepts. Cooperatives operate in 145 countries worldwide, contributing over $2.1 trillion to global trade. Governed by six core values and seven principles set by the International Cooperative Alliance (ICA) in 1895.

2.3 The Cooperative Movement in Sri Lanka: Began with the Ceylon Agricultural Association in 1904 and rural credit societies in Theldeniya in 1906. The Cooperative Societies Ordinance No. 7 of 1911 was enacted, followed by the establishment of the Cooperative Department on October 1, 1930. Multi-Purpose Cooperative Societies (MPCS) were formed in 1957, and People's Bank was established in 1961 by T.B. Ilangaratne and Philip Gunawardena, with Vincent Subasinghe as its first chairman ("Father of Sri Lanka's Cooperative Movement"). Operations are governed by the Cooperative Societies Act No. 5 of 1972.

3.0 Justification

Formerly, the cooperative was the "supermarket" of the village, selling essential foodstuffs and purchasing local grains like paddy, maize, and dried chilies, with entities like COOPFED, MARKFED, and CWE (Sathosa) emerging to support them.

However, with the open economy introduction in 1977, private supermarkets captured these opportunities. Due to a lack of government intervention, breakdown of apex bodies, political interference, and placing cooperatives under the 13th Amendment provincial subject framework, the movement fell into crisis.

Despite having nearly 2,500 active branches operating through hardships, the Ministry of Cooperative Development has identified the urgent need for structural, quantitative, and qualitative transformations to modernize the network.

4.0 Proposal & Store Hierarchical Network

The restructuring strategy is divided into three primary store models spanning from grassroots village levels to provincial capitals:

1. MiniCoop (14,022 Units)
Located in every Grama Niladhari Division (4,000–7,000 sq. ft.). Integrates a 500–750 sq. ft. Cooperative Rural Bank inside the premises.
2. CoopSuper (332 Units)
Located in every Divisional Secretariat Division (8,000–12,000 sq. ft.). Features agricultural purchase centers, banking, and air-conditioned retail.
3. CoopMega (25 Units)
Flagship stores at District / GA level (15,000–25,000 sq. ft.). Comprehensive multi-departmental store with EV charging stations and parking.
4.3.1 Proposed Departments across Outlets
  • CoopFresh: Fresh fruits, vegetables, and organic produce.
  • CoopMeat / Sea Fish: Fresh fish and meat.
  • CoopBakery: Bread and other flour-based products.
  • CoopMed: Pharmaceuticals at affordable prices.
  • CoopWine Stores: Wine and approved soft drinks.
  • CoopBeauty Store: Approved cosmetics and fragrances.
  • CoopHomeCare: Detergents, cleaning agents, kitchenware, linens, electrical appliances.
  • CoopEssentials: Grains, spices, oils, packaged foods.
  • CoopDairy: Milk, yogurt, ice cream, frozen foods.
  • CoopCorner: Cooperative branded products.
5.0 Supply Chain & Logistics Network

Centralized Category Management, establishment of 1 National Distribution Center + 5 Regional Centers equipped with cold chain facilities, direct purchases from farmer cooperatives, and long-term MSME partnerships to eliminate middleman price manipulation.

6.0 Digital Transformation

Integrated digital management system covering Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP), Point of Sale (POS), Warehouse Management System (WMS), Transport Management System (TMS), and Customer Relationship Management (CRM).

Includes online ordering apps, home delivery networks, click-and-collect counters, Loyalty Card rewards, and the nationwide rollout of the **CoopPay** digital payment platform.

7.0 Branding & Customer Experience (CX)

Implementation of a modern national cooperative supermarket visual identity, clean well-lit store layouts, customer service desks, automated ATMs, bill payment counters, Wi-Fi zones, and consumer-friendly product exchange policies.
